# Tracking Staple Food Price Trends in Selected Nigerian States

3MTT Capstone Project | Data Analysis and Visualization track
Author: [ADAMU SAMBO] | 3MTT ID: [FE/23/93905523]

## About
This project cleans and analyzes retail food price data from the World Food Programme (WFP) dataset for Nigeria, covering nine staples (rice, millet, sorghum, beans, groundnuts, onions, tomatoes and yam) from 2016 to 2026.

## Key findings
- Staple prices in Borno and Yobe rose between 144% (sorghum) and 855% (yam) from 2016 to 2025.
- Most staples are dearest between July and September.
- Lagos is the most expensive state; Kebbi, Adamawa and Jigawa are among the cheapest (2016-2022).
- Onions, yam and tomatoes are the most unstable; rice is the steadiest.
- Food prices moved closely with the exchange rate (correlation about 0.90), but this does not prove cause.

## Files
- food_price_analysis.xlsx: analysis workbook with cleaned data and summary tables
- clean_food_prices.csv: cleaned data used for the dashboard
- chart1 to chart5: the project charts

## Limitations
- The dataset is not nationwide. After 2023 it covers only a few states, mainly Borno and Yobe.
- Prices are in naira and are not adjusted for inflation.
- Correlation does not prove cause.

## Data source
World Food Programme, food prices dataset for Nigeria (Humanitarian Data Exchange).
